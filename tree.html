LOCAL ROOTS. GLOBAL REACH.

Why a team in Kosovo, and why this one

The honest case for nearshoring here, without the brochure language.

We work on your hours, EU or US

Our European clients get the full working day in CET. For US clients we run a shifted schedule so the team is online through the US morning and afternoon on the East Coast, and into the West Coast morning. You get live answers, not handover notes written for a team that's asleep.

Senior people at nearshore rates

Each of the three leads has 15 or more years of hands-on delivery, including national systems that are still in production. You get that level of design judgement on your project at rates well below Western Europe or the US, without a layer of account managers in between.

English is the working language

Every engineer works in English daily. Documentation, code review and meetings are in English by default; several of the team also work in German.

Built on public-sector discipline

We've spent years building systems for ministries, a national library and a privatisation agency, where audit trails, uptime and data handling aren't optional. That discipline comes with us to commercial work.

Your IP, your repo, your rules

Code is committed to your repositories from day one and belongs to you under the contract. We sign your NDA, use your access controls, and can work entirely inside your environment if you prefer.

Small enough that the founders are on your project

Fifteen engineers means Rrezart, Besart or Qendrim is in your weekly call, not a sales deck. If something is going wrong, you'll hear it from them first.

FROM CONVERSATION TO CODE

How an engagement starts

From first email to a working team in about a month. Each step has a clear exit if it isn't a fit.

  1. A 30-minute call

    You describe the work and the team you have. We tell you plainly whether we're the right fit and which model makes sense.

    Week 1

  2. A written proposal

    Named people, their CVs, the plan, the price, and the terms. No surprises in the contract that weren't in the proposal.

    Week 1 to 2

  3. A paid pilot

    Two to four weeks on a real, bounded piece of work. You see how we communicate, review and deliver before committing to more.

    Week 3 to 6

  4. Scale or stop

    If the pilot works, we grow the team to what you need. If it doesn't, you keep the code and the documentation and we part on good terms.

    After the pilot

A FEW THINGS TO KNOW

Questions we get before a contract

The ones that come up in almost every first call.

Who owns the code and the IP?

You do. The contract assigns all work product to you on payment. Code lives in your repositories from the first commit unless you ask us to host it.

What contracting entity do we sign with?

We contract with EU and US companies directly and invoice in EUR or USD. Details of the signing entity, VAT and payment terms are in every proposal.

How do you handle our data and access?

Least-privilege access through your identity provider where possible, no production data on personal machines, and an offboarding checklist when anyone leaves your project. Besart reviews the security setup of every engagement.

Can we interview the engineers first?

Yes. Every proposal names the people and includes their CVs. For dedicated teams and embedded engineers you interview them before anything is signed.

What hours do you work?

Whatever hours your team keeps. EU clients get a standard CET day. US clients get a team scheduled to overlap their working day, Eastern or Pacific, agreed in the proposal. Out-of-hours support can be arranged for production systems.

What if someone leaves the project?

You'll know as early as we do. We bring in a replacement with a handover period at our cost, and the founders stay constant across the engagement.

How do you communicate progress?

Your tools: Jira, Linear, Azure DevOps, GitHub, Slack or Teams. A short written update each week and a monthly review with the founder leading your account.

Do you work with startups?

Yes, when the work fits our three areas. We're a better fit for a funded company building a serious product than for a pre-product MVP sprint.
